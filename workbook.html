Getting Started

ABC
1Supplier Comparison Spreadsheet
2A practical workbook for comparing, evaluating and tracking Chinese suppliers.
4What this is
5Five working sheets and one list sheet. Use them in this order: find suppliers and record their quotes (Supplier Comparison), score the shortlist (Supplier Evaluation), line quotes up side by side (Quotation Comparison), then track samples (Sample Tracking) and keep a written record of every conversation (Contact Log).
6It is a spreadsheet, not a database: nothing is uploaded, nothing is shared, and no supplier can see what you type.
8How to use it
91. Read this sheet once. Then open Supplier Comparison and replace the two [DEMO] rows with your own suppliers.
102. Fill one row per quote, not one row per supplier. Two prices at two quantities are two rows.
113. Leave a cell empty rather than guessing. A blank is honest; an invented number is not.
124. Before you compare two unit prices, make the four conditions match: product specification, quantity, currency and Incoterm. The Quotation Comparison sheet checks this for you.
135. Shortlist three suppliers in Supplier Evaluation. Adjust the weights in row 4 to match what matters for your product — the weighted score recalculates.
146. When a sample arrives, log it in Sample Tracking and record the decision on the day you make it. You will not remember why in three weeks.
16What you fill in, and what is calculated
17You enterCalculated for you
18Supplier details, prices, MOQ, lead times, payment terms, freight and dutyLanded cost per unit (Supplier Comparison)
19Eight scores per supplier, and the weightsWeighted score 1–5 (Supplier Evaluation)
20Each quote, and one reference quote at the topGoods value, sample cost per unit, and whether a quote is comparable (Quotation Comparison)
21Sample dispatch and arrival datesDays in transit, and a flag when an expected sample is overdue
22Every conversation and the next actionDays since contact, and a flag when a follow-up is overdue
25The demo rows
26[DEMO] means the row is a fictional example written to show how the sheet works. The suppliers, prices and dates are invented. Delete both demo rows before you use the workbook — on the Comparison, Evaluation, Quotation, Sample Tracking and Contact Log sheets.
27Nothing in this workbook came from a real supplier, a real quotation or a real delivery.
29What this workbook deliberately does not do
30• It does not convert currencies. Comparing USD 1.85 with EUR 2.10 needs an exchange rate you choose and can defend — enter one currency, or convert outside the sheet and note the rate.
31• It does not look up duty rates, freight rates or lead times. Those come from your broker, your forwarder and your supplier.
32• It does not verify a supplier. A high score means you liked what you saw, not that the company has been audited, licensed or checked by anyone else.
33• It does not replace a contract, an inspection or legal advice.
35Sheet by sheet
36SheetWhat it is for
37Supplier ComparisonOne row per quote: who, what, how much, at what quantity and on what terms. Landed cost per unit is calculated.
38Supplier EvaluationEight criteria scored 1–5 with adjustable weights. Produces a weighted score that flows back to the comparison sheet.
39Quotation ComparisonLines quotes up against one reference quote and tells you which ones are actually comparable.
40Sample TrackingEvery sample: when it was requested, sent, arrived, what you found, and whether it was approved.
41Contact LogA dated record of every conversation and the next action, so nothing goes quiet.
42ListsThe dropdown values. Add to a list rather than typing over it.
45Two habits that matter more than the spreadsheet
46Ask for the quotation in writing, with the Incoterm and the quantity stated on it. And keep the approved sample sealed, labelled with the supplier and the date — that sample, not the spreadsheet, is what a dispute will be settled against.

48Free to use and share with your own team. Not legal, customs, tax or financial advice.
